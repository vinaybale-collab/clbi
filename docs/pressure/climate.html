Climate

Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse-gas emissions, in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent. We use Scope 2 as reported; most filings do not say whether it is location- or market-based. We do not score Scope 3.

BRSR P6 EI 7 asks for Scope 1 and Scope 2 totals (tCO2e) and intensities; Scope 3 is voluntary (LI 2). 473/500 companies have Scope 1+2 (13 with a reported zero component to confirm; 7 zero Scope 2 accepted as genuine; 8 held back). Scope 2 method stated in 16/465 filings (10 market, 6 location); Scope 3 in a resolvable unit in 141/465. Excluding held-back values, Scope 1+2 totals 1.33 bn tCO2e; NTPC 23.3%, top ten 65%, Power group 42%.

Key points

  • Scope 2 is de facto location-based: median implied factor 0.710 t/MWh, 286 of 433 companies exactly on the CEA FY2024-25 factor, although 441 of 465 filings do not state the method; the FY2025-26 factor (0.675) would lower these by about 5%.
  • A within-filing test (Scope 2 / purchased electricity) flags 42 companies; the final build's 1.5x-energy rule holds back 3, but 34 remain ranked (e.g. Nestle India 141 t/MWh).
  • Scope 2 is 3% of Scope 1+2 in Cement but 94% in Banks; 333 of 473 companies have more than half their footprint in Scope 2.
  • Ten companies emit 65% of the 1.33 Gt reported (excluding held-back values); NTPC alone 23.3%.
  • The ladder spans about 5,200x (Cement 1,020 to Insurance 0.19 tCO2e/Rs cr); Cement to IT services is 1,200x.

Reporting practice, contradictions and limits

What BRSR asks. Principle 6, Essential Indicator 7 asks for:

  • total Scope 1 and total Scope 2 emissions in metric tonnes of CO2e (with an optional gas-by-gas split);
  • their intensity per rupee of turnover, per rupee adjusted for purchasing-power parity, and per unit of physical output.

Scope 3 is a voluntary Leadership Indicator (SEBI BRSR format, Annexure II, July 2023). The form does not ask which Scope 2 method was used. It also does not ask whether the GHG boundary matches the financial boundary.

What companies report.

  • Coverage. 473 of 500 companies have a Scope 1 + 2 value. 13 of these carry a reported zero in Scope 1 or Scope 2 that is still to be confirmed. A further 7 report a zero Scope 2 that the final build accepts as genuine (for example Hindustan Unilever and BEL). 8 values are held back as data queries.
  • Scope 2 method. In the 465 parsed filings, only 16 say which method they used (10 market-based, 6 location-based). 8 mention both without assigning the total, and 441 say nothing.
  • Scope 3. 141 filings report Scope 3 in a resolvable unit, 155 in an ambiguous "MtCO2e" unit, and 169 not at all.
  • Concentration. Excluding the 8 held-back values, reported Scope 1 + 2 sums to 1.33 billion tCO2e. NTPC alone accounts for 309 Mt (23.3%), the ten largest emitters for 65%, and the Power group for 42%.

Boundary conditions that change the number.

  • Standalone vs consolidated. 22 of the 24 ranked peer groups mix the two bases.
  • The apparent effect is large but confounded. In Power, consolidated filers have a median of 1,060 tCO2e/₹ cr, against 6.4 for standalone filers. This is because the standalone filers are mostly hydro and transmission companies (NHPC, SJVN, POWERGRID), not because of the basis.
  • In transport the direction reverses: the standalone filers include the airline and the shipping companies.
  • The boundary effect cannot be separated from the business mix with these data.
  • Scope 2 method. Dividing Scope 2 by purchased non-renewable electricity gives the emission factor each company actually applied (433 companies).
  • The median is 0.710 tCO2/MWh, and the interquartile range is 0.710–0.712. 286 companies round to exactly 0.71.
  • This is the Central Electricity Authority's weighted-average grid factor for FY2024-25 (0.710, CO2 Baseline Database v21, CEA User Guide v21.0). The FY2025-26 factor, published in August 2026, is 0.675 (CEA User Guide v22.0).
  • So, although 441 filings do not say so, Scope 2 in BRSR is in practice location-based. It is computed with the previous year's factor, which is about 5% higher than the factor for the reporting year.
  • Captive power. When a company generates its own electricity from fuel, the emissions sit in Scope 1, while the electricity may still be reported as "non-renewable electricity consumed". That pushes the implied Scope 2 factor towards zero:
  • JSW Steel: 0.20 t/MWh on 5.15 TWh;
  • Chennai Petroleum: 0.012 t/MWh on 4.58 TWh;
  • NAVA: 0.0007 t/MWh on 0.83 TWh.
  • The split of emissions between Scope 1 and Scope 2 therefore depends on how the plant is owned, not on what it emits.
  • Utilities. Distribution-integrated utilities appear to put purchased power or network losses into Scope 2. Their implied factor is 26.5 t/MWh for Torrent Power and 6.4 for Tata Power, far above any grid factor.
  • Cross-company double counting. Scope 2 for the 500 (excluding held-back values) sums to 70 Mt. Much of that electricity is generated by companies in the same list, whose Scope 1 already counts it: the Power group's Scope 1 alone is 549 Mt. Totals across the index are therefore not national totals.

Contradictions (computed).

  • Scope 2 against electricity. 42 companies have an implied Scope 2 factor outside 0.5–1.0 t/MWh, and 34 of them are ranked in the final build. The extreme cases are:
  • more than 5 t/MWh: Nestlé India (141; ranked 16 of 27), Torrent Power (26.5), Eicher Motors (8.6), Kansai Nerolac (8.3), Canara HSBC Life (7.10, exactly ten times 0.71, which points to a factor-of-ten slip), Tata Power (6.4);
  • below 0.05 t/MWh: NAVA, Chennai Petroleum, Angel One.
  • The final build added a hold-back for a Scope 2 that implies more grid electricity than 1.5× total reported energy. It holds back three companies: Coal India, Canara HSBC Life and Ingersoll-Rand. The other extreme cases above remain ranked.
  • Scope 2 = 0 while buying grid power. Reliance Power (2.50 TWh of non-renewable electricity), Himadri Speciality Chemical (99,180 MWh), Lodha Developers (6,628 MWh) and United Breweries (5,067 MWh). The first three are not ranked. United Breweries' zero is among the 7 the final build accepts, and it is ranked 13th in FMCG.
  • Scope 1 = 0 while burning fuel. Azad Engineering reports 1,126,734 GJ of non-renewable fuel and zero Scope 1.
  • Impossible magnitudes.
  • Shyam Metalics reports Scope 1 + 2 of 79.0 Mt (8.6× its peer median per ₹). Its Scope 2 implies an electricity bill above revenue. It is held back.
  • Prime Focus reports Scope 1 + 2 of 2.31 Mt on ₹28 crore of revenue, with a Scope 2 that fails the same test. It is held back.
  • Scope 1 per GJ of fuel. Combustion alone gives roughly 0.06–0.10 tCO2/GJ: the 10th to 50th percentile is 0.061–0.093. Values far above this need process or fugitive emissions to be stated:
  • Coal India: 17.7 t/GJ, which requires large non-combustion emissions (for example mine methane) that the BRSR table does not label (its climate value is now held back on the Scope 2 test);
  • PCBL: 36 t/GJ;
  • Allied Blenders: 145 t/GJ;
  • Niva Bupa: 89 t/GJ.
  • The final build now flags 15 companies whose Scope 1 exceeds 0.12 t per GJ of non-renewable energy outside process-emission groups, with a "verify before naming" caution. Examples: Cupid 41.5, Swan Corp 1.14, Allied Blenders 0.75, Voltas 0.53, Cipla 0.39 t/GJ.

What the number can and cannot support.

  • It can rank operational (Scope 1 + 2) intensity within a peer group, where business models are alike.
  • It cannot:
  • rank across groups (the spread is 3–4 orders of magnitude);
  • be summed into a national total (double counting through electricity);
  • distinguish ownership of captive power from efficiency;
  • say anything about value-chain emissions. Scope 3 is usable for 141 of 465 filings.

Insights.

  1. Scope 2 is location-based in practice: the median implied factor is 0.710 t/MWh, and 286 of 433 companies sit exactly on the CEA FY2024-25 factor. The method is undeclared in 441 of 465 filings. Using the FY2025-26 factor (0.675) would lower every such Scope 2 by about 5%.
  2. A simple consistency test between two tables of the same filing (Scope 2 ÷ purchased electricity) flags 42 companies. The final build's new 1.5×-energy rule holds back three of them (Coal India, Canara HSBC Life, Ingersoll-Rand), but 34 remain ranked, including Nestlé India at 141 t/MWh.
  3. The heavy end and the light end are measuring different things. Scope 2 is 3% of the footprint in Cement and 94% in Banks, so a bank's climate rank is largely a rank of how much grid electricity it buys.
  4. Concentration is extreme: ten companies emit 65% of the 1.33 Gt reported (excluding held-back values), and NTPC alone 23.3%. Any index-level total or average is dominated by Power, which is itself 42% of the sum.
  5. The standalone/consolidated mix (22 of 24 groups) cannot be tested cleanly. The two bases coincide with different business models (hydro and transmission vs thermal; airlines vs ports).
