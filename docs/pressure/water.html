BRSR P6 EI 3 asks for withdrawal by five sources and total consumption; EI 4 discharge by destination and treatment; LI 1 (voluntary) per-facility data for areas of water stress with area name only. 473/500 companies have consumption. Withdrawal totals 14.5 bn kL, 81% by five companies (NTPC 41.6%); net consumption 3.98 bn kL, top five 51%. 57-subset: source table filled by 57/57, groundwater 36/57, seawater 8/57, water-stress values 20/57.

Key points

  • Water is the most concentrated pressure: five companies withdraw 81% of 14.5 bn kL; NTPC alone 41.6%; Adani Power's withdrawal is 83% seawater.
  • Consumption is not computed by one rule: 168/463 report consumption exactly equal to withdrawal (189 within 0.5%), many while also reporting discharge, 66 fail the balance by >10%.
  • The ladder spans about 660x (Power 2,401 to Insurance 3.6 kL/Rs cr); within Power consumption is 54% of withdrawal vs ~98-100% elsewhere.
  • Location is missing: only 20 of the 57-subset report water-stress values and none gives coordinates, so a scarcity price cannot be attached to company totals.

3

Reporting practice, contradictions and limits

What BRSR asks. Essential Indicator 3 asks for:

  • withdrawal by source (surface, ground, third-party, seawater/desalinated, other);
  • total consumption;
  • water intensity per rupee and per rupee adjusted for purchasing-power parity.

Indicator 4 asks for discharge by destination and level of treatment. Leadership Indicator 1 asks, for each facility in an area of water stress, for its withdrawal, consumption and discharge. It asks for the "name of the area" but not for coordinates (SEBI BRSR format).

What companies report.

  • Coverage. 473 of 500 companies have consumption.
  • Totals. Total withdrawal across the 500 is 14.5 billion kL. Five companies take 81% of it: NTPC alone 6.04 billion kL (41.6%), then Tata Power, Adani Power, Coal India and SAIL. Net consumption totals 3.98 billion kL, of which the top five take 51%.
  • Sources (57-company subset). All 57 fill the source table. 36 report groundwater, and 8 report seawater.
  • Water stress (57-company subset). 20 of 57 report any value in the water-stress table.

Boundary conditions that change the number.

  • Withdrawal vs consumption. CNPI uses reported consumption, capped at withdrawal, because the definitions differ so much. The median company reports consumption at 98% of withdrawal. 168 of 463 companies with both figures report consumption exactly equal to withdrawal (189 within 0.5%), and 148 report zero discharge. Power is the exception, at 54%.
  • Seawater and once-through cooling.
  • Adani Power: 805 million kL of its 966 million kL withdrawal (83%) is seawater (57-company subset).
  • Tata Power withdraws 3.70 billion kL but consumes 266 million kL, 14 times less.
  • A withdrawal-based index would put coastal thermal plants at the top for drawing seawater, which has no scarcity cost. A consumption-based index removes that, but then misses thermal discharge.
  • Seawater can also sit inside reported consumption. For the illustrative nature cost (set B), the final build removes seawater consumed from priced water for 7 companies (inputs/seawater_in_consumption.csv). These are Tata Power (200.5 million kL), Reliance (91.3 million), Adani Power (73.5 million), ONGC (18.4 million), NLC India (16.7 million), HPCL (6.4 million) and BPCL (0.8 million). The scored water value is unchanged.
  • Offices only. Banks, NBFCs and insurers report building water, and many use an assumption rather than a meter:
  • Canara Bank, Bandhan Bank, Bank of India and Shriram Finance each report discharge of exactly 80% of withdrawal.
  • Another 30 companies (24 of them ranked, 20 of them financials, for example HDFC Bank) report consumption at exactly 20% of withdrawal. The final build therefore scores their withdrawal instead (water_discharge_assumed).
  • This rule raises the office-based medians in the ladder below.
  • Location. The pressure of a kilolitre depends on the basin. The headline BRSR numbers are company totals with no location, and the water-stress table gives only an area name.

Contradictions (computed).

  • Consumption equals withdrawal while discharge is positive. In 49 companies the stated consumption ignores the stated discharge, so W − D ≠ C.
  • 9 of these report discharge at exactly 80% of withdrawal (for example Canara Bank, Bandhan Bank, Bank of India, Shriram Finance).
  • Union Bank of India reports consumption = withdrawal = discharge.
  • The water balance. For 66 of 454 companies the balance withdrawal − discharge − consumption fails by more than 10% of withdrawal.
  • Discharge exceeds withdrawal.
  • ITC: 1.92 billion kL discharged against 30.1 million kL withdrawn, 64×. This is retained in the parser and flagged.
  • Mahindra & Mahindra Financial: 17×.
  • Consumption more than 10× withdrawal (held back).
  • BEML: 44.8 million kL consumed against 0.45 million withdrawn.
  • Privi Speciality: 691 million against 0.76 million.
  • Bajaj Finance and Bajaj Finserv: consumption of 1.55 and 1.23 million kL against a withdrawal recorded as zero.
  • Consumption above withdrawal. 19 companies report this. CNPI caps it at withdrawal for 15 of them.

What the number can and cannot support.

  • It can support a within-group comparison of how much water a company makes unavailable, on its own definition.
  • It cannot:
  • say where the water was taken, so it cannot be priced by scarcity;
  • separate seawater from freshwater in the headline;
  • be trusted where consumption = withdrawal by construction (a third of filers);
  • support any statement on water quality: BRSR gives treatment level, not pollutant loads.

Insights.

  1. Water is the most concentrated pressure: five companies withdraw 81% of the 14.5 billion kL. NTPC alone withdraws 41.6%, much of the thermal-power withdrawal is cooling water, and Adani Power's is 83% seawater.
  2. "Consumption" is not computed consistently. 168 of 463 companies with both figures report consumption exactly equal to withdrawal (189 within 0.5%), many while also reporting discharge, and 66 of 454 fail the water balance by more than 10%.
  3. The intensity ladder spans about 660× (Power 2,401 kL/₹ cr; Insurance 3.6). Even within Power, consumption is only 54% of withdrawal, against about 98–100% almost everywhere else.
  4. Location is the missing variable. Only 20 of the 57-company subset report anything in the water-stress table, and none gives coordinates. A scarcity price, which Vinay wants for the nature-cost layer, cannot be attached to company totals.
