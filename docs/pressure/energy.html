3

Reporting practice, contradictions and limits

What BRSR asks. Essential Indicator 1 asks for:

  • electricity (A/D), fuel (B/E) and "other sources" (C/F), each split into renewable and non-renewable, in joules or multiples;
  • energy intensity per rupee of turnover, per rupee adjusted for purchasing-power parity, and per unit of physical output.

What companies report.

  • Coverage. 473 of 500 companies have a value.
  • Arithmetic. Every parsed filing that has a total also has renewable + non-renewable components summing to it within 1%.
  • Renewable share. Among the 380 real-economy companies with energy data, the median renewable share is 12.7%, and 65 companies are at 50% or more.
  • Unit slips. The XBRL is where unit errors live. For 10 companies the XBRL energy total differs from the filed PDF by a factor of 1,000 to 10¹²:
  • K.P.R. Mill: 10¹²;
  • Sobha, Bajaj Holdings, Firstsource, Tata Chemicals, JSW Energy, EID Parry, BASF India: 10⁹;
  • Godawari Power and Vardhman Textiles: about 10⁶;
  • Ratnamani: 10³.
  • CNPI corrected 38 energy values for 13 companies from the filed PDFs.
  • 4 further values remain held back as implausibly small: Thermax 0.0003 GJ, KPIT 0.00003 GJ, Gallantt Ispat 0.009 GJ, Choice International 0.001 GJ.

Boundary conditions that change the number.

  • Captive generation. A company that generates its own power can report the fuel (E) and also the electricity it consumes from that plant (D). Whether it does both is not stated anywhere in the form. The very low implied Scope 2 factors in §1 (JSW Steel, Chennai Petroleum) are the visible symptom.
  • Generators. For power generators, "energy consumed" is essentially fuel input converted into electricity sold to others. The Power median of 5,693 GJ/₹ cr is therefore a conversion measure, not end use, and the same energy reappears as other companies' grid purchases.
  • What counts as "renewable". Renewable energy is netted out of the pressure in full. This includes biomass and bagasse burnt in boilers (sugar, distilleries, paper) as well as contracted solar and wind. A rupee of renewable energy therefore lowers "energy" without any check on its land or air consequences.
  • Electricity share. The median electricity share of total energy ranges from 0.5% (Power), 6.6% (Steel) and 6.7% (Cement) to 93% (Banks) and 97% (IT services). For half the index, "energy" is essentially grid electricity.

Contradictions (computed).

  • Carbon per GJ of non-renewable energy. Across 457 companies with neither value held back, the median is 0.168 tCO2e/GJ, and the 5th–95th percentile range is 0.078–0.270. Outside this range:
  • Cupid: 41.5;
  • Swan Corp: 1.31;
  • Himadri Speciality Chemical: 0.98;
  • Allied Blenders: 0.82;
  • at the bottom, Azad Engineering: 0.0045 (zero Scope 1 against 1.22 million GJ of energy).
  • Coal India (1.87) and Canara HSBC Life (1.83) were also outliers; both are now held back.
  • Renewable share at the extremes. The group median is 0.1% in Power and 0.3% in Oil and gas, against 55.6% in IT services and 54.7% in FMCG. FMCG's high share comes largely from biomass fuels, a different thing from purchased green power, but the BRSR table does not distinguish them.
  • Held-back values. Held back for size: Eternal (463 GJ for a national delivery business) and Aditya Birla Real Estate (15.4 million GJ, 814× its peer median). The latter is a pulp-and-paper footprint filed under real estate.

What the number can and cannot support.

  • It can compare fossil-energy dependence within a peer group.
  • It cannot:
  • separate efficiency from captive-versus-grid ownership;
  • say anything about the land or air side of "renewable" biomass;
  • be compared between generators and users of electricity;
  • be read from the XBRL file without a unit check.

Insights.

  1. The XBRL is not machine-safe for energy: 10 companies' filed totals are wrong by factors of 10³–10¹² relative to their own PDFs. 38 values for 13 companies had to be corrected by hand.
  2. Heavy industry is a separate world: Power, Steel and Cement have medians of 4,258–5,693 GJ/₹ cr, and the services groups 0.9–6.2. That is three orders of magnitude, so cross-sector "energy leaders" are meaningless.
  3. Renewable share is high exactly where energy is small, and near zero where energy is large. The median is 55.6% in IT services against 0.1% in Power and 1.1% in Steel. Offsetting renewables in full therefore mainly reorders the light end of the index.
  4. The carbon-to-energy ratio (median 0.168 t/GJ) is a cheap and powerful consistency test. It isolates a handful of filings, from Azad's zero Scope 1 to Cupid's 41.5 t/GJ, that need a query before naming.
