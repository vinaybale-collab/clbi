Land and sites

BRSR has no field for land area. We ran an independent search of annual reports, mining-lease tables and environmental-clearance papers for all 500 companies. This page shows what it found. We report it as evidence and do not score it.

77companies with any land figure
13print a company-wide land total
252sites with an area, from 140 companies
10sites with printed coordinates

BRSR asks only for the number of plants/offices (Section A), a free-text table of operations in/around ecologically sensitive areas (P6 EI 11), EIAs (EI 12) and a voluntary biodiversity narrative (LI 3); no hectares or coordinates. Hermes search of 500, hand-classified in the final build: company-wide land held 13 (A), real-estate land banks 6 (R), partial/different boundary 7 (B), partial/older 51 (C), offices/no area 115 (D), none 308 (E); 187 name a site, 140 give a site area (252 sites, median 21 ha), 9 give coordinates (10 sites).

Key points

  • BRSR has no hectare field; after a full search and hand classification only 13 of 500 companies (2.6%) have a company-wide land-held figure, plus 6 real-estate land banks and 7 partial-boundary figures.
  • Sensitive-area evidence shrinks from 87 statements to 18 affirmative or hedged ones (13 companies), with 0 coordinates.
  • Coordinates exist for 10 of 326 sites, so GIS screening against WDPA/KBA, and hence the proposed MSA x proximity method, cannot yet be run.
  • Among the 13 category A figures land per revenue spans about 330x (Bharat Coking Coal 1.20 to Tech Mahindra 0.004 ha/Rs cr), and 3 of the 13 are on a different boundary from the BRSR.

Reporting practice and limits

What BRSR asks.

  • Section A asks for the number of plants and offices, national and international. It does not ask for their area.
  • Principle 6, Essential Indicator 11 asks for a table of operations or offices "in/around ecologically sensitive areas (such as national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, wetlands, biodiversity hotspots, forests, coastal regulation zones etc.) where environmental approvals / clearances are required": location, type of operations, and compliance.
  • Indicator 12 asks for environmental impact assessments of projects.
  • Leadership Indicator 3 asks for significant direct and indirect impacts on biodiversity in those areas.
  • There is no field for hectares, coordinates, land-use change or restoration area (SEBI BRSR format).

What companies report (independent Hermes search of all 500).

  • Company land figure.
  • The final build classifies these by hand (land_category; inputs/land_category_overrides.csv).
  • 13 companies have a company-wide figure for land held (category A).
  • 6 real-estate companies have a land bank (R).
  • 7 have a figure that covers a different or partial boundary (B): one facility, one lease, a subsidiary concession, or a licence area.
  • 51 have only a partial or older figure (C);
  • 115 are offices-only or give no area (D);
  • 308 disclose no area (E).
  • Sites. 187 companies name at least one site, 140 give a site area (252 sites), and 9 companies give coordinates for 10 sites.
  • Sensitive-area statements. The "near a sensitive area" field holds 87 statements from 59 companies. On reading them:
  • 31 are denials or "not near" ("No national park/wildlife sanctuary…", "The Company has no operations/offices in/around ecologically sensitive area");
  • 14 are "none stated";
  • 4 are off-topic (one repeats the BRSR question; one is about leases);

Boundary conditions that change the number.

  • Field type. Owned, leased, mining-lease and land-bank figures are different quantities:
  • Oil India's 482,884 ha is a petroleum mining-lease (licence) area, not land held, and is now category B;
  • real-estate land banks include joint-development land at partial economic interest (Prestige).
  • Land boundary vs BRSR boundary. The two often differ, even within category A:
  • JSW Steel's land figure is consolidated (India operations including subsidiaries) while its BRSR is standalone;
  • Tech Mahindra's is standalone (India, 13 locations) against a consolidated BRSR;
  • Acutaas' covers all group sites, including a Korean joint venture, against a standalone BRSR.
  • In category B, Grasim's figure is drawn from UltraTech's sites against a standalone BRSR, and NAVA's 36,000 ha is a Côte d'Ivoire subsidiary concession.
  • Period. Of the 326 site rows, 175 are dated FY2025-26 and 37 FY2024-25; the rest are undated, dated by their environmental clearance, or web-sourced.
  • Earlier estimates disagree.
  • The legacy NII pilot carried 101,900 ha for Coal India and 36,600 ha for NTPC, but the new search finds no current company figure for either.
  • For HAL the legacy 2,630 ha compares with 4,860 ha now; for BEL, 1,385 ha with 1,245 ha (freehold plus leasehold, converted from acres); for BHEL, 13,250 ha with 6,755 ha.

What the evidence can and cannot support.

  • It can support case studies and a list of named, sourced sites.
  • It cannot support:
  • any rank;
  • any sector comparison;
  • any spatial overlay with protected areas: 10 coordinates for 326 sites, and none for an affirmative sensitive site.

Insights.

  1. The BRSR has no hectare field, so land is the one pressure that has to be built from outside the BRSR. After a full search and hand classification, only 13 of 500 companies (2.6%) have a company-wide figure for land held. A further 6 have real-estate land banks, and 7 have figures for a partial or different boundary.
  2. The sensitive-area evidence is much thinner than it first appears: 87 statements reduce to 18 affirmative or hedged ones from 13 companies, with 0 coordinates. A filled field is usually a denial, a blank or a placeholder.
  3. Site areas exist (252 sites, median 21 ha), but coordinates exist for 10. Without points or polygons, no GIS screening against the World Database on Protected Areas or Key Biodiversity Areas is possible, so the proposed MSA × proximity method cannot yet be run.
  4. Even among the 13 category A figures, three are not on the BRSR boundary (JSW Steel, Tech Mahindra, Acutaas). Any land-per-revenue ratio would need its own boundary reconciliation.
