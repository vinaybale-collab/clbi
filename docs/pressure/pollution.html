BRSR P6 EI 9 asks for waste generated in 8 categories (incl. other hazardous), recovered by 3 routes and disposed by 3 routes. 472/500 companies have a value; median recovery 82%; 114/465 recover >=99%. Coal India reports 5.35 bn t (91.3% of the 5.85 bn t total) and recovers all but ~2,700 t. 57-subset: categories filled 57/57, hazardous positive 43/57.

Key points

  • Waste is defined by what companies include: Coal India 5.35 bn t vs GMDC 69 t; NTPC 57,193 t vs Reliance Power 106.7 million t; Godrej Properties 2.91 million t vs Anant Raj 0.74 t; pollution stays unscored in Power, mining, construction and real estate.
  • The offset is near-total: 114/465 recover >=99%, 54 have net waste <= 0, Cement's net median is 0.2% of gross.
  • 72/449 filings do not balance and 21 recover more than they generate; stock movements need their own line.
  • Hazardous waste is already a BRSR category and is filled (57/57 in the subset), so a hazard-weighted pressure is feasible once the parser reads it.

3

Reporting practice, contradictions and limits

What BRSR asks. Essential Indicator 9 asks for waste generated by category, in metric tonnes:

  • plastic; e-waste; bio-medical; construction and demolition; battery; radioactive;
  • other hazardous; other non-hazardous;
  • with intensity per rupee and per unit of output.

It then asks for waste recovered (recycled, re-used, other recovery) and disposed (incineration, landfill, other).

What companies report.

  • Coverage. 472 of 500 companies have a value.
  • Recovery. The median company recovers 82% of what it generates. 114 of 465 report recovering 99% or more.
  • Waste categories (57-company subset). All 57 fill the categories; 43 report positive "other hazardous" waste and 50 report e-waste.
  • One company dominates the total. Coal India reports 5.35 billion tonnes, mostly overburden, which is 91.3% of all waste reported by the 500 (5.85 billion t). It records 5.35 billion t as recovered, leaving a net of about 2,700 t.

Boundary conditions that change the number.

  • Mineral wastes in or out. Fly ash, overburden, tailings and construction debris are in or out at the company's discretion:
  • NTPC reports 57,193 t of waste, against 106.7 million t for Reliance Power and 16.8 million t for Adani Power;
  • in mining, Coal India reports 5.35 billion t, while GMDC (a lignite miner) reports 69 t;
  • in construction, Larsen & Toubro reports 596,194 t, while IRCON reports 9.7 t, NBCC 24 t and RVNL 33 t;
  • in real estate, Godrej Properties reports 2.91 million t and Brigade 2.02 million t (largely construction and excavation material), while Anant Raj reports 0.74 t.
  • This is why the final build does not score pollution in these four groups: Power, Non-ferrous metals and mining, Construction and engineering, and Real estate.
  • Recovery from stock. 21 companies report recovering more than they generated in the year. Examples: Hindalco (11.4 Mt recovered against 9.6 Mt generated), Adani Power, Torrent Power, Patanjali Foods, Godrej Industries, J.K. Cement. This happens when legacy ash or stock is drawn down. Net waste is then negative or zero, and 54 companies have net waste ≤ 0.
  • "Recovered" is not an outcome measure. The recovery category includes fly ash sent to cement and bricks and overburden used for backfill. These may be genuine circular uses, but a near-100% net offset rewards the category choice as much as the practice.
  • Net vs gross. In Cement the median gross is 13.2 t/₹ cr and the median net is 0.022: the offset removes 99.8%.

Contradictions (computed).

  • Generated vs recovered + disposed. In 72 of 449 companies these differ by more than 10%. Examples:
  • NMDC: 21.4 million t generated, 350 t recovered, 2,623 t disposed;
  • Jindal Saw: 13.9 million t generated, 0.25 million t accounted for;
  • Happy Forgings: 30,257 t generated, 4 t accounted for.
  • Recovered more than 1.5 × generated (held back). Indraprastha Gas (7.8×), Metro Brands (2.3×), Privi Speciality (2.2×), TVS Motor (1.6×).
  • Gross figures more than 300× the Power peer median (held back). Reliance Power, Jaiprakash Power, JSW Energy, NLC India, Adani Power.
  • Hold-back cases with very small figures. RVNL (33 t), NBCC (24 t), IRCON (9.7 t), CONCOR (1.5 t) and GMDC (69 t) are all held back as far below their peer medians.

What the number can and cannot support.

  • It can support a within-group comparison of non-mineral waste intensity in manufacturing and services.
  • It cannot:
  • compare mining, power, construction or real estate;
  • say anything about toxicity: hazardous waste is reported in BRSR but is not separated in CNPI's pressure;
  • treat "recovered" as harmless;
  • be summed. One company is 91% of the total.

Insights.

  1. Waste is defined by what companies choose to include: 5.35 billion t at Coal India against 69 t at GMDC, 57,193 t at NTPC against 106.7 million t at Reliance Power, and 2.91 million t at Godrej Properties against 0.74 t at Anant Raj. Pollution must stay unscored where mineral or construction wastes are material, as it now is in four groups.
  2. The offset is nearly total: 114 of 465 companies recover 99% or more, 54 have net waste ≤ 0, and Cement's net median is 0.2% of its gross. A net-waste rank mostly rewards the "recovered" label.
  3. 72 of 449 filings do not balance (generated ≠ recovered + disposed by more than 10%), and 21 recover more than they generate. Stock movements need their own line.
  4. The BRSR category table already separates hazardous waste, and the 57-company subset shows it filled throughout. A hazard-weighted pressure is feasible from existing filings, once the parser reads the categories.
