Takeaways

Gas logistics and distribution companies sit at the top and upstream producers at the bottom. Larger companies rank worse (correlation between revenue and score −0.40).

Takeaways

  • ONGC (13th), Oil India (12th) and Reliance Industries (11th) fill the bottom. Aegis Logistics is the highest-ranked like-for-like company (92.0, 1st). Petronet LNG (2nd) carries a naming caution for a waste value 149 times below the median; Gujarat Gas ranks 3rd (82.1).
  • Reliance's standalone filing accounts for 38.8% of the group's Scope 1+2 and 45.2% of its water. IOC adds 22.0% of emissions.
  • Water intensity spans 226 times, from 1.7 kL per ₹ crore (Petronet LNG) to 390 (Reliance). Climate spans 62 times.
  • The median renewable share is 0.35%, and Scope 2 makes up a median 8% of emissions. Gross and net ranks agree at 0.98.
  • Gujarat Gas reported water use as a flat 20% of withdrawal; CNPI scores it on withdrawal, and it holds 3rd place.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI holds back Indraprastha Gas, which reports recovering more than 1.5 times the waste it generated and carries a water value 258 times below the median. CNPI also holds back the Adani Total Gas and IGL air figures. Vedanta Oil & Gas reports no pressures.
  • Air: 9 of 13 report in tonnes.
  • Land: Oil India's 482,884 ha is the area of 25 petroleum mining leases (FY2024-25). Companies should report lease area and surface footprint as separate figures.

Caveats

  • The group puts exploration and production, refining, LNG regasification, gas transmission, city-gas distribution and LPG logistics in one table.
  • Twelve of 13 report standalone.

Illustrative nature cost

The group’s median 15.5% of EBITDA (n = 13), 2.1% of revenue. Median company shares by pressure: carbon 72.8%; energy 12.6%; water 12.3%; waste 0%; air 1.3%. No rank uses these figures.
