Takeaways

With pollution unscored, Anant Raj is the highest-ranked like-for-like company and DLF ranks last. Real estate gives the best land disclosure of any group: 6 of 12 report land banks.

Takeaways

  • CNPI does not score pollution here because developers report construction waste on different bases: DLF reports 236,462 t generated, Anant Raj 0.74 t. Anant Raj leads the like-for-like ranking (85.8), ahead of Godrej Properties (83.3) and Oberoi Realty (66.7).
  • DLF ranks last (6.7). It emits 41.5% of the group's Scope 1+2 and uses 53.3% of its water. Its unscored waste (27.3 t per ₹ crore, 5.5% recovered) makes up 82% of the group's unrecovered waste.
  • Climate intensity spans 25 times (Godrej Properties 0.93, DLF 23.3 tCO2e per ₹ crore) and water 26 times (Oberoi 33.6, DLF 873 kL).
  • Six companies report land banks: Lodha 1,578 ha, Sobha 709, Prestige 393, Brigade 223, Signatureglobal 193 and Anant Raj 130. These figures count land held for development.
  • Larger developers rank worse (correlation between revenue and score −0.32). The median renewable share is 8.7% (Prestige highest, 35%), and Scope 2 passes half of emissions for all nine.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI holds back Prime Focus, whose Scope 2 implies an electricity bill larger than its revenue, and Aditya Birla Real Estate (climate 345 and energy 814 times the median). Aditya Birla Real Estate also carries a reviewed caution: its pulp-and-paper footprint does not match the group.
  • Lodha stays unranked. It reports zero Scope 2 with 84% renewable electricity, below the 90% CNPI needs to accept a zero.
  • Air: 3 of 9 report. None of the 11 listed sites has coordinates.

Caveats

  • Developers book revenue when projects complete, so per-rupee intensity swings from year to year. Mall operators such as Phoenix Mills (₹544 crore standalone revenue, 8th) run a different business from developers.
  • Seven of the nine ranked companies report consolidated.
