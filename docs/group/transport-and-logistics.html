Takeaways

InterGlobe Aviation emits 78% of the group's Scope 1+2 yet ranks 3rd. CNPI scores the airline on water and waste against ports and couriers.

Takeaways

  • InterGlobe emits 77.6% of the group's Scope 1+2 and uses 79.8% of its non-renewable energy. It ranks 6th of the 9 companies scored on climate, first on water, and 3rd overall (58.9).
  • Dropping any one pressure can move ranks a lot (minimum leave-one-out rank correlation 0.81, the lowest of all groups). Revenue-based and profit-based views agree at 0.43, also the lowest.
  • The two shipping lines rank last (Shipping Corporation 7th, Great Eastern 8th). Great Eastern has the highest climate intensity, 221.6 tCO2e per ₹ crore, 85 times GMR Airports' 2.6.
  • GMR Airports is the highest-ranked like-for-like company (78.1, 1st). It draws 83.2% of its energy from renewables, the one member above half. Blue Dart ranks 2nd (64.7).
  • Adani Ports uses 62.1% of the group's water, scores zero on water and drops from 3rd on gross figures to 5th net. Delhivery reported water use as a flat 20% of withdrawal; CNPI scores it on withdrawal, and it ranks 6th.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI holds back Container Corporation's waste figure (1/2,500 of the median). Aegis Vopak Terminals reports no pressures.
  • Air: 5 of 8 report; CNPI holds back GMR's air figure.
  • Land: JSW Infrastructure gives the one company-wide figure (1,303 ha), though ports and airports occupy large areas. Adani Ports' Mundra statement puts the nearest bird sanctuary 48 km away.

Caveats

  • The group joins an airline, two shipping lines, ports, an airport operator and courier firms. Aviation fuel dominates the group total.
  • Delhivery and Blue Dart hire trucking contractors, whose emissions fall in Scope 3, which CNPI excludes.

Illustrative nature cost

The group’s median 14.6% of EBITDA (n = 9), 1.6% of revenue. Median company shares by pressure: carbon 59.9%; energy 8.8%; water 15.1%; waste 0%; air 0%. No rank uses these figures.
