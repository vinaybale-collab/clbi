Takeaways

Solar-module and optical-fibre makers sit at the bottom. Waaree Energies produces 62% of the group's unrecovered waste and 36% of its water use.

Takeaways

  • Climate intensity spans 68 times: 0.59 tCO2e per ₹ crore at Netweb Technologies, 40.4 at Sterlite Technologies. The middle half sits between 3.1 and 8.3, with a long upper tail.
  • The two solar PV makers rank 21st (Waaree) and 23rd (Premier Energies) of 24. They draw 5.3% and 6.6% of their own energy from renewables. Waaree accounts for 16.5% of group emissions, 35.6% of water consumption and 62.0% of net waste.
  • Scope 2 makes up a median 88% of emissions and passes half for 22 of 24, yet the median renewable share is 8.5%. Hitachi Energy (93.9%) and GE Vernova T&D (72.1%) stand apart; CNPI accepts their zero Scope 2 on 100% renewable electricity. Hitachi Energy climbs from 16th on gross figures to joint 7th net, the biggest gain in the group.
  • Kaynes recovers 4% of its waste and drops from 6th on gross figures to joint 12th net; Dixon (0.2%) drops from 9th to 15th. TD Power Systems returns 76% of the water it withdraws and climbs from 11th to 5th.
  • Pressures here move together (climate–water rank correlation 0.59). Dropping any one pressure leaves a rank correlation of at least 0.96. The highest-ranked like-for-like companies are Netweb (94.6), Honeywell Automation (87.0) and Inox Wind (81.5).

What better disclosure would change

  • 5 of 29 members stay unranked. ABB India, Siemens and Siemens Energy India have no revenue matched to the BRSR year; Aditya Infotech and Emmvee report none of the four pressures.
  • Hitachi Energy (joint 7th) carries a naming caution: its Scope 1 of 7,263 t works out to 0.27 t per GJ of non-renewable energy, more than its reported fuel could emit. Air: 10 of 24 ranked companies report emissions in tonnes.
  • Land: Emmvee, an unranked company, gives the one company-wide figure (9.08 ha). One of the 15 listed sites has coordinates.

Caveats

  • The group mixes cable makers, contract electronics assemblers, wind, solar and grid-equipment makers and computing-system vendors. Contract assemblers (Dixon, Kaynes, Syrma) book large pass-through revenue, which lowers their per-rupee intensity.
  • Members come from four NSE sectors (Capital Goods 18, Consumer Durables 3, Telecom 2, IT 1).

Illustrative nature cost

The group’s median 2.6% of EBITDA (n = 25), 0.4% of revenue. Median company shares by pressure: carbon 75.9%; energy 6.2%; water 17.6%; waste 0.2%; air 0%. No rank uses these figures.
