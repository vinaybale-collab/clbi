Takeaways

IRCON, RVNL and NBCC take the top three places. Their waste figures sit about 670–1,400 times below the peer median, which fits work done by subcontractors.

Takeaways

  • IRCON is the highest-ranked like-for-like company (93.9). Water intensity spans 430 times, from 0.71 kL per ₹ crore (RVNL) to 306 (IRB Infrastructure); climate spans 59 times and energy 132 times. IRB scores zero on all three scored pressures.
  • Larsen & Toubro emits 55.7% of the group's Scope 1+2, uses 55.9% of its non-renewable energy and 53.7% of its water, and ranks 7th of 12 (42.4).
  • The three scored pressures move together (climate–water rank correlation 0.92). Dropping any one leaves the order close to unchanged (minimum correlation 0.97).
  • Offsets make little difference. The median renewable share is 2.4% and the highest 11.6% (Engineers India). Net and gross ranks agree at 0.99, and companies move 0.2 places on average.
  • Eleven of 12 report returning some water (median 19% of withdrawal), the most consistent water-return reporting of any industrial group.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI does not score pollution here. Companies report construction and demolition debris on different bases: RVNL, NBCC and IRCON report almost none, while L&T reports about 600,000 tonnes.
  • Air: 2 of 12 report in tonnes. Central Mine Planning & Design Institute reports no climate, energy or water data.
  • Land: four companies report office sites and no other land. L&T makes the group's sole sensitive-area statements: parts of its Hazira jetties and Kattupalli yard fall inside coastal regulation zones.

Caveats

  • NBCC, IRCON and RVNL subcontract site work, so their contractors carry the site emissions in their own Scope 1. Self-performing EPC firms carry those emissions themselves.
  • IRB Infrastructure (a toll-road developer and operator) and ITI (telecom equipment and projects) sit at the edge of this group.
