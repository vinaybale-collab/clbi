Takeaways

Pipe and tube converters that buy their steel take four of the top five places. Jindal Stainless (3rd) is the highest-ranked steel maker, and the three integrated majors emit three-quarters of the group's Scope 1+2.

Takeaways

  • APL Apollo (1st), Ratnamani (2nd), Tube Investments (4th) and Welspun Corp (5th) carry like-for-like cautions: their suppliers carry the steel-making emissions in their own Scope 1. Jindal Stainless (81.4) and Tata Steel (60.2, 6th) are the highest-ranked like-for-like companies.
  • Climate intensity spans 309 times, from 6.4 tCO2e per ₹ crore (APL Apollo) to 1,963 (Sarda Energy). The median of 463 is the second-highest of any group.
  • Tata Steel (27.0%), JSW Steel (24.0%) and SAIL (22.8%) emit 73.9% of the group's Scope 1+2. SAIL uses 43.5% of the group's water.
  • Offsets make little difference. The median renewable share is 1.1% (Tube Investments highest, 31.7%); gross and net ranks agree at 0.97, and companies move 0.5 places on average.
  • Jindal Saw recovers 1% of its waste and holds 34.0% of the group's unrecovered waste. Eight of 15 recover at least 90%.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI holds back Shyam Metalics because its Scope 2 implies an electricity bill larger than its revenue. It also holds back Gallantt Ispat's energy figure (near zero) and Ratnamani's air figure.
  • KIOCL does not state its BRSR boundary.
  • Land: JSW Steel (8,380 ha, India operations), NMDC Steel (728 ha, its only plant) and the unranked Shyam Metalics (739 ha) give company-wide figures. NAVA's 36,000 ha manganese concession in Côte d'Ivoire covers a subsidiary. NMDC Steel states that its Nagarnar plant sits close to reserved forest.

Caveats

  • Read pipe and tube converters apart from steel makers. CNPI moved Godawari Power & Ispat and others in from NSE Capital Goods.
  • NAVA's rank carries a 'discontinued operations' flag.
