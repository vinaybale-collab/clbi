Takeaways

Hospitals sit close together (2.5 times on climate), so one value can move a rank. No hospital recovers most of its waste.

Takeaways

  • Climate intensity runs from 4.9 tCO2e per ₹ crore (Apollo Hospitals) to 12.2 (Narayana Hrudayalaya); energy spans 2.5 times. Narayana and Global Health tie for last (21.9).
  • The median hospital recovers 18% of its waste, and none reaches 90%. Indian rules require hospitals to treat and dispose of biomedical waste, which limits recycling.
  • Dr Lal PathLabs, a diagnostics chain, is the highest-ranked like-for-like company (84.4, 1st), with the lowest energy and water intensity. Apollo ranks highest among in-patient hospitals (78.1) while emitting 27.5% of the group's Scope 1+2.
  • Scope 2 makes up a median 89% of emissions; renewables a median 20% (Narayana highest, 36%). Rainbow Children's Medicare returns 43% of its water and climbs from 7th on gross figures to 5th net.
  • Water shows the widest spread, 4.6 times (Dr Lal 51.5, Aster DM 239 kL per ₹ crore). Aster DM scores zero on water.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI holds back Apollo Hospitals' air figure, 3,200 times the peer median, as a probable unit or boundary error.
  • Narayana Hrudayalaya (joint 8th) carries a naming caution: its Scope 1 of 21,898 t is more than its reported fuel could emit. Dr Agarwal's Health Care reports none of the four pressures, and 5 of 9 report air.
  • Land: no company gives a land figure. Companies list 14 sites (19.2 ha), none with coordinates.

Caveats

  • CNPI compares diagnostics labs with in-patient hospitals, whose energy and water use differ in kind.
  • Six of nine report consolidated and three standalone.

Illustrative nature cost

The group’s median 4.3% of EBITDA (n = 9), 1% of revenue. Median company shares by pressure: carbon 47.6%; energy 3.4%; water 49.5%; waste 0.3%; air 0%. No rank uses these figures.
