Takeaways

Government wholesale lenders take the top three places: REC, the highest-ranked like-for-like company, then PFC and IRFC. A per-rupee yardstick favours large loan books run from few offices.

Takeaways

  • IRFC's climate intensity (0.0055 tCO2e per ₹ crore) is 357 times below IFCI's (1.95). Branch-heavy retail lenders fill the bottom: the microfinance lender CreditAccess Grameen (28th) and the gold-loan company Manappuram (29th, 7.3).
  • Water intensity spans 2,176 times (IRFC 0.059, CreditAccess 128.8 kL per ₹ crore), the widest of any group.
  • Eleven of 29 report recovering all their waste. Those 11 tie on a pollution score of 82.3, so the pressure separates few companies.
  • Eight lenders, including PFC (2nd) and IRFC (3rd), reported water use as a flat 20% of withdrawal; CNPI scores them on withdrawal. CreditAccess Grameen and Manappuram belong to this set and rank last.
  • This group has the least concentrated footprint: the top three emitters (Muthoot 16.1%, Shriram 11.4%, Cholamandalam 10.6%) hold 38%. The median renewable share is 0.1%; L&T Finance draws 74.8%.

What better disclosure would change

  • CNPI holds back Bajaj Finance and Bajaj Finserv: each reports water consumption more than 10 times its withdrawal. Home First reports no waste, and Authum reports zeros.
  • Lenders should measure water discharge and stop assuming it.
  • Air: 5 of 29 report. All 33 members report office sites and no other land, and none discloses financed exposure to land use.

Caveats

  • CNPI compares wholesale infrastructure financiers, consumer lenders, housing financiers and microfinance lenders on revenue. Per-rupee intensity rewards balance-sheet size over operational efficiency.
  • Twenty-two report standalone and seven consolidated.

Illustrative nature cost

The group’s median 0.04% of revenue. Median company shares by pressure: carbon 52.1%; energy 4.2%; water 44%; waste 0%; air 0%. No rank uses these figures.
