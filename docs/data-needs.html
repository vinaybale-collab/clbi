What data a corporate nature index needs

The index works with what BRSR provides. This page works the other way round: for each parameter, the data a defensible index would need, the standard that defines it, what BRSR asks today, how much of it was observed, and the single change that would close most of the gap.

The full note

CNPI v1.2 is built from what India's BRSR already provides. This note works the other way round. For each environmental parameter it sets out:

  • the data a defensible corporate nature index would need;
  • which international standard defines that data;
  • what the BRSR provides today;
  • how much of it we actually observed in the CNPI v1.2 dataset;
  • and the gap.

A single summary table ("one sheet") closes the note and is also saved as data_needs.csv.

How "observed coverage" was computed. Counts come from the following sources:

  • outputs_v12/ (500 companies);
  • the parsed BRSR XBRL (465 filings);
  • the Hermes land and site search (500 companies, 326 site rows);
  • a 57-company subset for BRSR sub-tables the parser does not normalise. This is the readable part of a truncated full-fact audit file, running alphabetically from 360ONE to BALKRISIND. It is not a random sample, and its counts are labelled "(57-subset)".

Nothing is estimated or filled.

1. Climate (GHG)

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Scope 1, by source (combustion, process, fugitive) and by gas tCO2e Company; site for large emitters
Scope 2, location-based, naming the grid factor and its version tCO2e Company
Scope 2, market-based, with the contractual instruments used tCO2e Company
GHG consolidation approach (operational control / financial control / equity), matched to the revenue boundary text Company
Scope 3 by category tCO2e Company

Standards. IFRS S2 (location-based Scope 2 required); ESRS E1-6 (location- and market-based); GRI 305-1 to 305-3 (GRI 102 from 2027); TNFD refers GHG to IFRS S2.

BRSR today.

  • EI 7: Scope 1 and Scope 2 totals in tCO2e, with an optional break-up by gas, and intensities.
  • LI 2: Scope 3 (voluntary).
  • There is no field for the Scope 2 method, the grid factor, the consolidation approach, or the split of Scope 1 by source.

Observed in CNPI.

  • 473/500 companies have Scope 1 + 2. 460 of these have no unconfirmed zero component (13 do; a further 7 zero Scope 2 values are accepted as genuine).
  • The Scope 2 method is stated in 16/465 filings.
  • The implied factor sits at 0.71 t/MWh (the CEA FY2024-25 factor) for 286/433 companies.
  • 42 companies fail the Scope 2 ÷ electricity consistency test. The final build's new rule (Scope 2 implying grid electricity above 1.5× total energy) holds back 3 of them, and 34 remain ranked.
  • Scope 3 is reported in a resolvable unit by 141/465 (another 155 use an ambiguous "MtCO2e" unit).

Gap.

  • Medium. The headline number exists almost everywhere.
  • What is missing is the metadata that makes it comparable: the method, the factor, the boundary, and the process/fugitive split.
  • Scope 3 is missing for most companies.

2. Energy

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Fuel consumed, by fuel type GJ (and t or kL) Company; site
Electricity purchased from the grid MWh Company
Electricity self-generated, with the fuel already counted MWh Company
Renewable electricity, split into on-site, contracted and certificate-backed MWh Company
Biomass fuel, with its source GJ, t Company

Standards. GRI 302 (GRI 103: Energy 2025 from 2027); ESRS E1-5; BRSR Core "energy footprint".

BRSR today.

  • EI 1: electricity, fuel and "other" sources, each split into renewable and non-renewable.
  • Intensities: per rupee, per rupee adjusted for purchasing-power parity, and per unit of physical output.
  • There is no field separating captive generation from grid purchase, and no split by fuel type.

Observed in CNPI.

  • 473/500 companies have energy.
  • The electricity/fuel split is present in 461/465 parsed filings.
  • 10 filings have XBRL totals wrong by 10³–10¹² relative to the filed PDF; CNPI corrected 38 values for 13 companies by hand.
  • Captive generation cannot be identified in any filing. The low implied Scope 2 factors at JSW Steel (0.20), Chennai Petroleum (0.012) and NAVA (0.0007) are the visible symptoms.

Gap.

  • Medium. The quantities exist.
  • The structure (captive vs grid, fuel type) and the machine-readable units do not.

3. Water quantity

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Withdrawal by source: surface, ground, third-party, seawater, produced m³ (kL) Site
Discharge by destination m³ Site
Consumption, computed as withdrawal − discharge ± change in storage m³ Site
Freshwater vs other water (TDS > 1,000 mg/L), for both withdrawal and discharge m³ Site

Standards. GRI 303-3, 303-4, 303-5; ESRS E3-4; TNFD C3.0 (consumption defined as withdrawal less discharge, citing GRI 303-5).

BRSR today.

  • EI 3: withdrawal by five sources, and total consumption.
  • EI 4: discharge by destination and level of treatment.
  • Everything is reported at company level only.

Observed in CNPI.

  • 473/500 companies have consumption.
  • The source table is filled by 57/57 companies in the 57-subset.
  • 175/454 report consumption exactly equal to withdrawal, 49 of them while also reporting discharge.
  • 30 companies (20 of them financials) report consumption at a fixed 20% of withdrawal. The final build scores their withdrawal instead.
  • 66/454 fail the water balance by more than 10%.
  • 2 report discharge greater than withdrawal (ITC at 64×).

Gap.

  • Medium. The fields exist, but consumption is not computed by one rule.
  • No site figures are reported.

4. Water source location and water stress

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Facility coordinates, or at minimum district and river basin lat/long; text Site
Withdrawal and consumption at each facility m³ Site
Water-stress classification, naming the dataset used (for example WRI Aqueduct baseline water stress, or Central Ground Water Board block category) class Site
Groundwater share at each facility % Site

Standards.

  • TNFD C3.0: "Water withdrawal and consumption (m³) from areas of water scarcity, including identification of water source".
  • GRI 303-3-b and 303-5-b: water stress.
  • ESRS E3-4: consumption in areas with water stress.
  • SBTN freshwater: basin-level targets.

BRSR today.

  • LI 1 (voluntary): for each facility in an area of water stress, the "name of the area", the nature of operations, and the withdrawal, consumption and discharge.
  • There are no coordinates and no stated definition of water stress.

Observed in CNPI.

  • Water-stress values are reported by 20/57 (57-subset).
  • Facility coordinates: 0 in BRSR.
  • From the Hermes site search: district is known for 280 of 326 named sites and state for 314. This is enough for district-level screening of named sites only.
  • Groundwater is used by 36/57 (57-subset); its median share of withdrawal is 24%.

Gap.

  • High. This is the variable that turns a kilolitre into a pressure, and the one CNPI's planned scarcity price needs.
  • BRSR makes it voluntary and does not define "water stress".

5. Wastewater and water pollution

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Discharge volume by receiving body, freshwater or other m³ Site
Pollutant loads: BOD, COD, total N, total P, heavy metals, as relevant t/yr Site
Treatment level class Site
Pollutants released to soil, by type t Site

Standards. TNFD C2.1 wastewater discharged (including concentrations of key pollutants) and C2.0 pollutants released to soil; GRI 303-4; ESRS E2-4.

BRSR today.

  • EI 4: discharge volume by destination and treatment level (none, primary, secondary, tertiary).
  • There is no pollutant-load field and no soil-release field.

Observed in CNPI.

  • Total discharge is in 463/465 parsed filings, and the destination/treatment tags are present in 57/57 (57-subset).
  • Pollutant loads: 0 (not asked).
  • Not used in CNPI.

Gap.

  • High. Volume without load does not measure pollution.
  • The State Pollution Control Board consent and monitoring data hold loads, but not in the BRSR.

6. Waste, including hazardous waste

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Waste generated, hazardous and non-hazardous, by stream t Company; site for mining and power
Named mineral streams (fly ash, overburden, tailings, slag, red mud, construction and demolition) on their own lines t Company
Diverted from disposal (reuse, recycling, other) and directed to disposal (incineration, landfill, other), each split by hazard class t Company
Recovery from stock or legacy deposits, separate from current-year arisings t Company
Plastic placed on the market and its recyclability t Company

Standards. GRI 306-3, 306-4, 306-5; ESRS E5-5 (hazardous and radioactive waste); TNFD C2.2 waste generation and disposal (hazardous and non-hazardous) and C2.3 plastic pollution; BRSR Core "embracing circularity".

BRSR today.

  • EI 9: eight categories (plastic, e-waste, bio-medical, construction and demolition, battery, radioactive, other hazardous, other non-hazardous).
  • Recovered by three operations, and disposed by three operations.
  • There is no line for stock recovery, and no named mineral streams.

Observed in CNPI.

  • 472/500 companies have waste.
  • The category table is filled by 57/57 in the 57-subset; 43/57 report positive other-hazardous waste.
  • 21 companies recover more than they generate.
  • 72/449 do not balance (generated ≠ recovered + disposed by more than 10%).
  • Mineral and construction waste is reported so inconsistently that pollution is unscored in 4 groups: Power, Non-ferrous metals and mining, Construction and engineering, and Real estate. Examples: Coal India 5.35 billion t against GMDC 69 t; NTPC 57,193 t against Reliance Power 106.7 million t; Godrej Properties 2.91 million t against Anant Raj 0.74 t.

Gap.

  • Medium. A hazard split exists and is filled.
  • What is missing: a rule for mineral streams and stock movements. CNPI's parser does not yet read the categories.

7. Air pollutants (non-GHG)

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
NOx, SOx, PM2.5/PM10, VOC (NMVOC) and NH3, as annual mass t/yr Site
Sources covered: process, captive power, diesel generator sets, fugitive dust text Site
Measurement method: continuous monitoring (CEMS) or calculation text Site

Standards. TNFD C2.4 "Non-GHG air pollutants (tonnes) by type": PM2.5 and/or PM10, nitrogen oxides, VOC/NMVOC, sulphur oxides and ammonia. GRI 305-7; ESRS E2-4.

BRSR today.

  • EI 6: NOx, SOx, PM, POP, VOC, HAP and "others", each with "please specify unit".
  • There is no mass requirement, no PM size fraction and no NH3.

Observed in CNPI.

  • 245/500 companies have NOx + SOx + PM in tonnes, and 178 are ranked.
  • 111 more report in non-mass or undeclared units (excluded).
  • 10 companies with more than 1 Mt of Scope 1 report no air tonnage.
  • POP, VOC or HAP entries exist for 41/57 (57-subset), with units unverified.

Gap.

  • High. "Please specify unit" defeats comparability.
  • Requiring tonnes per year would bring up to 111 more companies into use (from 245 to as many as 356), with little new measurement where continuous emission monitoring already exists.

8. Land use and land-use change

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Total area controlled or managed: owned, leased, mining lease ha Company and site
Disturbed area (cumulative and in the year) ha Site
Rehabilitated or restored area ha Site
Land converted in the year, by prior ecosystem type and new use ha Site
Site boundary: polygon for sites of 10 ha or more, point otherwise GIS Site

Standards.

  • TNFD C1.0 total spatial footprint (km²): area controlled or managed, plus disturbed area, plus rehabilitated or restored area.
  • TNFD C1.1 extent of land/freshwater/ocean-use change (km²), by ecosystem type and business activity.
  • GRI 101-5 (site location and size in ha).
  • SBTN Land: no conversion, land footprint reduction, landscape engagement; polygons for sites of 10 ha or more.

BRSR today.

  • Section A: the number of plants and offices.
  • EI 12: environmental impact assessments of projects.
  • There is no area field of any kind.

Observed in CNPI (Hermes search, outside the BRSR).

  • In the hand-classified final data, 13/500 companies have a company-wide figure for land held (A).
  • 6 have real-estate land banks (R), and 7 have figures for a partial or different boundary (B).
  • 51 more have only partial or older figures (C), making 77 with any figure.
  • 140 companies give an area for at least one site, covering 252 sites (median 21 ha).
  • Restoration area is found for 53 companies, and greenbelt area for 37 sites.
  • Disturbed area and land converted in the year: not found as standard fields.
  • Even within category A, 3 of the 13 land figures are on a different boundary from the BRSR (JSW Steel, Tech Mahindra, Acutaas).

Gap.

  • Critical. The BRSR does not ask, so all land data must be assembled by hand from annual reports and environmental clearances, with mixed periods and boundaries.

9. Biodiversity: sensitive sites and state of nature

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
Site coordinates or polygon GIS Site
Distance to, or overlap with, protected areas, KBAs, Ramsar sites, eco-sensitive zones and tiger reserves (from a named dataset) km; ha of overlap Site
Area of natural ecosystem on site, and change in the year, by type ha Site
Ecosystem condition class, and change class; ha Site
Threatened species (IUCN Red List and national lists) affected count Site
Restoration area and method ha Site

Standards.

  • TNFD C5.0 ecosystem extent, C5.1 ecosystem condition, C5.2 species extinction risk.
  • GRI 101-5 and 101-7 (the latter replacing 304-1 and 304-4).
  • ESRS E4-5 (2023 version: number and area of sites in or near protected areas or KBAs).
  • SBTN Step 1 (site locations required).

BRSR today.

  • EI 11: a table of operations or offices "in/around ecologically sensitive areas" giving location (free text), type of operations and compliance. There are no coordinates, no distance, no area, and no dataset named.
  • LI 3 (voluntary): narrative on significant direct and indirect impacts.

Observed in CNPI.

  • The EI 11 table is filled by 15/57 (57-subset), and 6 of those 15 entries are "NA".
  • The LI 3 text is present for 43/57 (57-subset); 30 of these are "not applicable" or deny any impact.
  • In the Hermes reading of all 500, 87 sensitive-area statements reduce to 18 affirmative or hedged statements from 13 companies. None of these sites has coordinates.
  • Coordinates exist for 10 of 326 sites.
  • External comparison: IIM Bangalore finds that 75.97% of the top 1,000 have no credible biodiversity disclosure, while 80.04% report operations in or around sensitive areas (Tripathi et al. 2026).

Gap.

  • Critical. Without coordinates nothing can be screened independently. The self-declared field is mostly denials.

10. Value chain: commodities and Scope 3

Data required.

Field Unit Granularity
High-impact natural commodities sourced, by type (for example coal, iron ore, bauxite, limestone, timber and pulp, palm oil, soy, cotton, sugarcane, cattle, fish) t Company, by origin state or country
Share sourced under a sustainable-management plan or certification % Company
Scope 3, by category tCO2e Company

Standards.

  • TNFD C3.1: quantity of high-impact natural commodities (tonnes), split by type, and the proportion under a sustainable-management plan or certification.
  • IFRS S2 and ESRS E1-6: Scope 3.
  • SBTN Step 1: upstream locations.

BRSR today.

  • LI 2: Scope 3 (voluntary).
  • Principle 2: sustainable sourcing as a percentage of inputs (no tonnes, no origin).
  • There is no commodity-tonnage field.

Observed in CNPI.

  • Scope 3 is reported in a resolvable unit by 141/465 filings.
  • Commodity tonnes by origin: 0 (not asked).

Gap.

  • Critical for FMCG, textiles, paper, food, and banks' financed footprint.
  • CNPI's own operations-only scope understates these sectors by construction.

Reading the sheet.

  • Severity. Critical means the BRSR does not ask and nothing usable exists. High means it asks loosely, or voluntarily, and coverage or comparability fails. Medium means the number exists but its metadata or boundary prevents clean comparison.
  • The five asks with the largest return are the ones about location and units: site coordinates, water by facility in stressed areas, land area, air in tonnes, and Scope 2 method.
  • What the asks cost companies. Most companies already hold this information in environmental-clearance and consent documents, so the asks are about formatting, not new measurement.
  • Biodiversity and land use. Coordinates alone would let an index do the GIS overlay itself, instead of relying on self-declared proximity.
