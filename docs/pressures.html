How the pressures were read

Draft for internal review, 28 October 2026. Not for publication. Refreshed on the final CNPI v1.2 build (outputs_v12, built 2026-10-28T12:01:19Z; 409 companies ranked in 24 groups).

This note reads the CNPI v1.2 data backwards. Instead of asking which company ranks where, it takes one environmental parameter at a time and asks five questions: what the BRSR asks for and what companies actually put in it; which boundary choices change the number; how far apart sectors sit on intensity; where the data contradict themselves; and what the number can and cannot carry.

Sources and method. Every number below is computed with pandas from the files in outputs_v12/ (scores, company data, data queries, land evidence, land sites). The parsed BRSR XBRL for 465 companies (normalized_companies.json) is also used. Where a figure comes from elsewhere, the source is cited with its URL. Three conventions apply throughout:

  • Ladders are medians of intensity per ₹ crore of revenue (Revenue from Operations, on the company's own BRSR basis), by peer group.
  • Each ladder includes only companies with a positive value, a verified revenue figure, and no open data query on that element.
  • A group is shown only if at least 6 companies qualify. The land list in §6 follows its own rule.
  • Financial-services groups show only the operational footprint (offices and data centres).
  • "Implied factor" checks compare one reported table with another inside the same filing: for example, Scope 2 against purchased non-renewable electricity.
  • They use the raw XBRL sub-fields.
  • They are restricted to the 447 companies whose XBRL total energy agrees, within 2%, with the value CNPI finally used. This excludes companies whose XBRL energy was corrected from the PDF.
  • "57-company subset." Sub-fields that the parser does not normalise were read from the only surviving full-fact audit file:
  • water by source, water-stress tables, waste categories, the ecologically-sensitive-area table, and the biodiversity text;
  • the file is truncated; its readable part covers 57 companies, alphabetically from 360ONE to BALKRISIND;
  • these counts describe that subset only and are not a random sample.

Cross-cutting observations

  • Ladder spans (median intensity, highest to lowest group):
  • climate about 5,200×;
  • energy about 6,200×;
  • water about 660×;
  • net waste about 740,000× (gross about 450,000×);
  • air about 600,000×.
  • Only within-group ranking is defensible.
  • The same few groups recur at the top. Power and Steel are in the top three of the climate, energy, water and air ladders, and Cement is in the top three of climate, energy and air. Non-ferrous metals and mining tops waste and is second in water.
  • Within-filing consistency tests catch errors the outlier tests miss. The tests are Scope 2 ÷ electricity, CO2 ÷ energy, the water balance and the waste balance. Together they flag:
  • 42 Scope 2 cases;
  • 66 water-balance cases;
  • 72 waste-balance cases;
  • almost all of them inside the 300× hold-back line, and many of them ranked. The final build's new rules (Scope 2 above 1.5× energy; Scope 1 above 0.12 t/GJ) now catch part of the first two families.
  • The fields a nature index most needs are absent from the structured XBRL headline. These are location (facility coordinates), water source and stress, hazard class and land area. Where they do exist in the filing, they sit in sub-tables or free text.

What a defensible corporate nature index would need, parameter by parameter, is set out in the data needs page.
